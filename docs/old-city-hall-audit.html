UX audit · Quick audit (prototype run)

Old City Hall Barbecue & Brewery

A restaurant visitor usually wants one of five things: see the menu, order, book a table, check the hours, or find the place. This audit asks how quickly the site gets them there, on a phone and on a desktop.

oldcityhalloswego.com18 Nov 202611 pages × 2 viewportsaxe-core 4.10 + manual heuristic review

Verdict

The brand and food photography are strong, and every page loads cleanly. The problem is that the site reads like an SEO article where it should work like a restaurant site. The things guests come for (order, reserve, today's hours) are pushed below long keyword-heavy paragraphs, and on mobile there's no persistent way to act.

Fix these three first:

  1. Put Order and Reserve in the hero, and add a sticky action bar on mobile.
  2. Show today's hours, the address and a tap-to-call number at the top of every page.
  3. Re-enable pinch-zoom, a one-line code change that fixes a critical accessibility failure on all pages.

Findings, ranked

#1

The main actions are buried, and there's no way to act while scrolling on mobile

CriticalFlow + heuristic
Evidence
The desktop hero has no button. The four key actions (Order Catering, Order Takeout, Menu, Reservations) first appear about 1,500px down, as small, equal-weight white buttons. On mobile, "Order Takeout" sits 1,952px down, the header doesn't stay on screen as you scroll, and the page is about 18 screens long.
Why it matters
Restaurant visitors arrive with a task, usually ordering or booking. Every scroll before the action costs orders, especially on phones. Nielsen #7, flexibility and efficiency of use.
Fix
  • Hero: one primary button (Order Takeout) and one secondary (Reserve a Table).
  • Mobile: a sticky bottom bar with Order, Reserve, Call and Directions (shown in the mockup above).
  • Make Order the visually dominant action, since it's the money path.
#2

Hours, address and phone appear only in the footer

CriticalHeuristic
Evidence
Hours are listed only in the footer, about 18 screens down on mobile. The restaurant is closed Monday and Tuesday, but that's stated nowhere near the top. The home page has no tel: link at all.
Why it matters
"Are they open now?" is the most common restaurant question. A guest who drives over on a Monday becomes a bad review. Nielsen #1, visibility of system status.
Fix
Add a slim strip under the header on every page: Open today 11:30am–8pm · 159 Water St · Call. Have it say "Closed today, opens Wednesday 11:30am" on Mondays and Tuesdays.
#3

Pinch-zoom is disabled on every page

CriticalRule · axe meta-viewport
Evidence
All 11 pages ship maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=no, so axe-core flags a critical WCAG 1.4.4 (resize text) failure.
Why it matters
Guests reading a menu on a phone, especially older guests, can't zoom into small italic dish descriptions.
Fix
<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1">
#4

Text set directly on busy photos is hard to read

HighVisual
Evidence
The Wednesday deal on mobile puts white body text directly on mac and cheese and a burger, with no overlay. The desktop hero puts a 90-word paragraph on the photo. Automated contrast checkers can't measure text over images, so this one needs a human eye.
Why it matters
The promo is a revenue driver, and the price and terms are the hardest part to read.
Fix
Put the text on a solid panel (as the desktop version already does), or add a dark gradient behind it. Keep text on photos to a headline and a button.
#5

Ordering is split across three platforms with no guidance

HighFlow
Evidence
Takeout and catering go to Incentivio, delivery goes to DoorDash, and reservations go to Toast (in a new tab). The Wednesday $12 deal is DoorDash-only, but the main "Order Takeout" button goes to Incentivio. The top-level "ORDER TO GO" item links to #.
Why it matters
A guest who wants the Wednesday deal for pickup can't tell where to go, and may order at full price or give up. Nielsen #4, consistency and standards.
Fix
Make one "Order" entry point that opens a simple chooser: Pickup (order direct), Delivery (DoorDash), Catering. Say which deals apply where.
#6

The navigation repeats "Catering" three times and mixes in a second business

HighInformation architecture
Evidence
"Catering" appears under Order To Go, Menus ("Catering & To-Go Orders") and Events. The mobile menu opens with every submenu expanded, giving a list of 20+ items. Apartments, a separate residential business, sits in the restaurant's main nav. Two URLs also serve the same page (/catering and /pages/catering).
Fix
  • One Catering destination.
  • Collapse the mobile accordions by default.
  • Move Apartments to the footer or a small top utility link.
  • Redirect duplicate URLs.
#7

Keyword-heavy copy slows scanning, and the Menus page buries the menu

MediumContent
Evidence
Headings like "One of the Best Restaurants Oswego NY Locals Recommend" and paragraphs that repeat "Oswego NY" read as written for search engines. The Catering page has 6 <h1> tags and Events has 5. On mobile, the first dish on the Menus page appears after about 3.7 screens of intro.
Fix
On Menus, lead with the dishes and move the SEO copy below them. Use one H1 per page and short, plain section intros. Local SEO is better served by the Google Business Profile, a meta description and schema markup than by repeated keywords.
#8

The contact form fights mobile users

MediumForm · inspected, not submitted
Evidence
  • Email and phone fields are type="text", so phones show the wrong keyboard.
  • No autocomplete, so autofill doesn't work.
  • No field is required.
  • The message box is one line tall.
  • The "not for reservations" note comes after the Submit button.
Fix
<input type="email" name="email" autocomplete="email" required>
<input type="tel" name="phone" autocomplete="tel">
<textarea rows="5" required></textarea>
Move the reservations note above the form and link it to the booking page.
#9

Hero carousel

MediumHeuristic
Evidence & fix
The home hero is a carousel with arrow controls. Few visitors move past the first slide, and the moving images compete with the headline. Use one strong image and put any other photos in the gallery.
#10

Menu typography is hard to read

MediumVisual
Evidence & fix
Dish descriptions are set in a letter-spaced italic serif, and the dietary legend (GF, mGF, DF, mDF) is a block of italic text. Use the upright body face for descriptions, put prices in a consistent right-aligned column, and use small dietary badges.
#11

No meta descriptions

LowRule
Evidence & fix
None of the 11 pages has a meta name="description", so Google and social shares show arbitrary snippets. Write one 150-character description per page.
#12

Embedded frames have no titles

LowRule · axe frame-title
Evidence & fix
The Google Map, the events widget and four 3D apartment tours are untitled iframes, so screen readers announce them as "frame". Add title="Map to Old City Hall" and similar to each.
